Build an interactive, highly detailed 3D treetop village as a single self-contained HTML file.

## Technical constraints
- One HTML file only. All HTML, CSS, and JavaScript inline.
- Use three.js version 0.170.0 loaded via an import map from https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.170.0/ (including addons such as OrbitControls from the same version). No other external libraries.
- No external assets: every model, texture, and material must be generated procedurally in code (geometry, canvas-generated textures, or shaders).
- It must run by opening the file directly in a current desktop Chrome browser with zero console errors.
- Target a steady 60fps on a typical modern laptop with default settings. Use instancing, merged geometry, or level of detail where appropriate.

## The scene
A village built high in a forest of giant trees, connected by rope bridges.
- At least 7 giant trees with realistic-looking trunks (bark texture, roots spreading over the forest floor, branching limbs) and dense layered canopies.
- At least 12 dwellings built on platforms around and between the trunks: wooden plank walls, shingled or thatched roofs, windows, doors, railings, chimneys or stovepipes with rising smoke. Dwellings should vary in size, shape, and height.
- Rope bridges connecting platforms, with individual planks, rope railings, and a visible sag. Bridges should sway gently.
- Spiral staircases or ladders running down some trunks to the forest floor.
- Additional structures: a central communal platform with a larger hall, a lookout tower at the top of the tallest tree, and a rope-and-pulley lift.
- Forest floor: ferns, moss, fallen logs, mushrooms, rocks, a small stream with moving water.
- Props that reward close inspection: lanterns, hanging laundry, flower boxes, barrels, crates, birdhouses, wind chimes.
- Life: small animated villagers (original simple stylized figures) walking along bridges and platforms on believable paths, birds flying between trees, falling leaves or drifting particles.

## Level of detail
The scene must hold up at every zoom level:
- From far away: a readable silhouette of the whole village in its forest.
- Mid range: individual buildings, bridges, and villagers are clearly distinct.
- Up close (camera right next to a doorway or bridge): planks, ropes, window frames, and props are individually modeled, not just textured flat surfaces.

## Camera and controls
- Orbit mode (default): orbit, pan, and zoom with mouse and trackpad, smoothly damped, with sensible limits (cannot go below the ground).
- First-person walk mode: WASD plus mouse look, with the ability to walk along platforms and bridges at their height.
- A key or button to toggle between modes, and a "reset view" button.
- An on-screen help overlay listing controls, which can be dismissed.

## Parameter panel
A clean, collapsible control panel (build it yourself, no UI libraries) with live updates:
- Time of day (slider, 0 to 24h): sun position, sky color, light color, and shadows change accordingly. At night, lanterns and windows glow and emit light.
- Season (spring, summer, autumn, winter): canopy color and density, ground cover, and particles change (for example falling leaves in autumn, snow in winter).
- Fog density.
- Wind strength: affects canopy sway, bridge sway, and smoke direction.
- Number of dwellings.
- Number of villagers.
- Random seed with a "regenerate" button that produces a different but equally coherent village layout.
- Shadow quality (off, low, high).
- A small FPS counter.

## Quality bar
- Art direction should be cohesive, warm, and storybook-like, not a collection of default primitives with flat colors.
- Lighting should be considered: soft shadows, ambient occlusion feel, atmospheric depth.
- The code should be well organized into clear sections or functions, with brief comments.

Output only the complete HTML file.
